## The First Thing You Notice Is Not Speed. It's Silence.

A properly waxed chain does not sound lubricated.

It sounds absent.

That silence is not magic. It's friction reduction. Not witchcraft. Watt-craft.

Zero Friction Cycling, widely regarded as the most exhaustive independent drivetrain testing authority in the world, has consistently found that hot melt wax outperforms wet lubricants for both friction and wear across their full 6,000km block testing, which covers clean, dry, wet, and extreme contamination conditions.

Adam Kerin of Zero Friction Cycling has noted that a poor lubricant choice or a contaminated chain can cost upwards of 5 watts at 250 watts, and that number increases as power increases.

Friction Facts testing, reported through VeloNews, showed wax-based systems running nearly 6 watts faster than standard oil lubricants after one hour in dirt and water.

I've used Manchester to London as my real-world testing ground across three different drivetrain setups. Here's what actually happened.

### Chain 1: Manchester to London, 2016 — "The Experiment"

![Crossing the finish line at Rapha Manchester to London 2016](../../assets/blog/uploads/28895328483_0cd4bdd42f_o.jpg)

Ten years ago, immersive waxing was fringe territory. When I told my friend Liam Maybank, founder of Aerevolution and a legitimate UK timetrial engine, that I was riding M2L, he offered to prep my chain for £20. He promised durability and a few free watts.

I wasn't convinced. But I trusted his judgement, so I agreed.

The process looked like a scene from Breaking Bad. Solvents. Home oven. Serious kitchen chemistry. It was not what anyone would call accessible. But I showed up on start day with a freshly waxed chain.

Then the forecast flipped. The 2016 edition became a washout. I panic-bought a Rapha waterproof Shadow jersey and bib shorts the day before. If my drivetrain was going to be waterproof, so was I.

The moment I clipped in, something felt different. Not faster. Quieter.

I rode off the front with two others early: Jake and Tom. After a few hours in the rain, their drivetrains sounded like they were grinding gravel. Mine stayed silent. That silence became fuel. I rode them off my wheel and spent the next six hours alone. Low position. Rhythm. Efficient pedalling.

When I reached London, my legs were finished.

**My drivetrain was not.**

## Sa Calobra: When 18 Seconds Becomes Personal

Manchester to London proved durability.

Sa Calobra proved the margin.

9.4 kilometres. 7% average gradient. Mallorca's most iconic climb.

![Sa Calobra mountain road in Mallorca, iconic cycling climb with dramatic switchbacks](../../assets/blog/uploads/image-2-landscape-sa-calobra-gareth-winter-sub-30.webp)

Climbing it in under 30 minutes became a number I couldn't let go of. I'd tried before. 30:47. Then 30:21. Then 30:12. Each time close enough to taste. Each time, far enough to hurt.

When you miss a target by 12 seconds, closing the final 18 stops being theory. It becomes personal.

The maths is straightforward. At roughly 300 watts sustained climbing, a 3-watt saving represents around 1% of total output. One percent over 30 minutes is approximately 18 seconds. Eighteen seconds is the difference between unfinished business and closure.

![Climbing Sa Calobra in Mallorca during sub-30 minute hill climb effort](../../assets/blog/uploads/image-1-hero-sa-calobra-gareth-winter-sub-30.webp)

For the sub-30 attempt, I controlled everything. Weight. Tyre pressure. Pacing. Sleep. Fuel. Position. And friction.

Fresh immersion in Secret Chain Blend, optimised for outright efficiency. Super Secret Drip applied the night before. No drivetrain contamination. No boundary friction penalty. No excuses.

On previous attempts, somewhere around minute 18, doubt arrived. Cadence dropped. Torque increased. Resistance crept in. When you're at threshold, drivetrain losses stop being abstract numbers. You feel them.

That day, everything felt direct. Clean. Mechanically quiet.

When I crossed the line and saw 29:53 on the clock, it wasn't relief.

**It was validation.**

![Road cyclist in a skinsuit holding a high end race bike](../../assets/blog/uploads/dscf3576.jpg)

Wax didn't give me lungs. It didn't give me legs. But it removed resistance at the metal interface. Marginal gains stack. And tungsten disulfide is not marginal.

## Is Chain Waxing Right for You?

![Gareth Winter road cyclist racing a time trial with a skinsuit and aerodynamic helmet](../../assets/blog/uploads/gareth-winter-time-trial.webp)

**The Watt-Conscious Racer.** If 3–5 watts at race power means something to you, if you've ever stared at a power file looking for the place you lost time, then drivetrain friction is a variable you can actually control. Most can't say the same about their legs on race day.

**The Long-Distance Endurance Rider.** You're covering big distances. Cost per kilometre matters. Component longevity matters. A waxed chain running 10,000 kilometres means fewer cassette replacements, fewer chain replacements, and less maintenance time overall.

**Anyone Running High-End Components.** Campagnolo Super Record. Shimano Dura-Ace. SRAM Red. If you're investing in premium drivetrain performance, the lubricant you use determines how long that investment lasts. Oil accelerates wear. Wax slows it.

**The Rider Who's Just Tired of the Mess.** Oil is reactive. You clean because it got dirty. Wax is proactive. You prevent contamination from adhering in the first place. Your drivetrain stops feeling disposable and starts feeling like the precision instrument it is.
